When to Order: turning sales history into a reorder rhythm

A retail owner was ordering stock on gut feel: sometimes too late, sometimes too much. We turned two years of sales history into reorder points the whole team can follow.

IndustryRetail
Decision areaInventory Planning
Published2026

The question

"How much should I stock, and when should I reorder, so I stop losing sales to empty shelves without tying up cash in stock that sits?"

The owner ran a busy retail store with hundreds of product lines. Fast movers ran out before the next delivery; slow movers filled the storeroom. Every order was a judgement call, and the judgement was under pressure.

The data

Two years of point-of-sale exports: transactions, product lines, quantities, and dates. No new system was installed and no manual logging was added. The raw exports were cleaned and combined into one documented dataset, the foundation for everything that follows.

The finding

23% of lost salestraced to just 12 fast-moving products that repeatedly ran out in the same week of each month

The stockouts were not random. Demand followed a monthly rhythm the ordering schedule ignored. Meanwhile, a long tail of slow movers was reordered out of habit, locking up cash that the fast movers needed.

RM31k in freed cashidentified in slow-moving stock that could be run down without hurting sales

The action

Each product line received a reorder point and a suggested order quantity, calculated from its own demand pattern and updated as new sales come in. The owner's team now orders from a list, not from memory. Holiday peaks are planned weeks ahead instead of discovered at the till.
